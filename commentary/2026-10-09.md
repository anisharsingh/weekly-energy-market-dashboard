## What changed and why

1. WTI edged up $0.74 to $91.85 and Brent rose $2.47 to $104.72, which widened the spread to $12.87, and this comparison is like-for-like because Brent was already on the December contract.
2. Hurricane Isaias shut in roughly 72% of US Gulf offshore oil output, about 1.5 million barrels per day, and a little under 60% of offshore gas output, which supported prices late in the week.
3. Offsetting that, Trump said talks with Iran were productive and that Washington would not launch more attacks before the midterms, and China plans to resume refined-product exports after its Golden Week holiday, both of which eased supply fears.
4. Western Canadian Select's discount to WTI widened to about $25.55, the widest since 2023, which reflects record tanker rates and difficulty moving heavy crude overseas.
5. EIA's inventory and storage data for this week come out Oct 14 and 15, so next week I'm watching how fast Gulf production returns, whether the hurricane damaged Gulf Coast refineries, and where the Iran talks go.
