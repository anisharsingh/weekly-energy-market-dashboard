## What changed and why

1. WTI edged up $0.74 to $91.85 and Brent rose $2.47 to $104.72, widening the Brent-WTI spread to $12.87; with both already on their current contracts, the comparison is like-for-like.
2. Hurricane Isaias shut in roughly 72% of US Gulf offshore oil output, about 1.5 million barrels per day, and nearly 60% of offshore gas output, which supported prices late in the week.
3. The shut-ins showed up in EIA's report on Oct 15: commercial crude fell 8.6 mb to 415.6 mb, the biggest draw since February and 3% below the five-year average, while refinery utilization rose 0.8 points to 93.5%.
4. Cushing stocks rose 1.9 mb and gasoline rose 3.5 mb, but distillates fell 2.5 mb to 102.6 mb, about 12% below the five-year average, so the tightness was in crude and diesel rather than gasoline.
5. Gas storage rose 67 Bcf to 3,567 Bcf, 45 Bcf above the five-year average but 23 Bcf short of the average injection, which narrowed the surplus from 2.0% to 1.3%; I'm watching whether Gulf output recovers and whether crude keeps drawing with the SPR unchanged near 283 mb.
