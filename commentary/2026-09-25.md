## What changed and why

1. WTI fell $7.89 (about 8%) to $92.41 while Brent finished slightly higher at $104.32, so the Brent-WTI spread widened from $3.57 to $11.91.
2. WTI dropped as hopes for a US-Iran truce removed some geopolitical premium, and part of the weekly decline is a contract roll, because the November contract became the prompt WTI contract at a lower price than October's close.
3. Brent held up because supply risks persisted, including Houthi attacks on Saudi targets and refinery disruptions in Russia, while talk of a possible US diesel export ban pushed the spread to its widest since May on expectations that US refiners might run less crude if they could not export diesel.
4. US commercial crude rose 0.9 mb to 427.3 mb, Cushing rose to about 24.3 mb and refinery utilization fell to about 92.5%, all consistent with softer domestic demand for crude.
5. Gas storage rose 64 Bcf to 3,415 Bcf, and next week I'm watching whether the diesel export ban talk resolves, since it directly affects the WTI discount, and how the Brent contract roll affects the weekly comparison.
