## What changed and why

1. WTI slipped $1.30 to $91.11 while Brent was essentially flat like-for-like (+0.11% per Reuters), even though its reported settle fell $2.07, because Brent rolled from the November to the December contract during the week.
2. The driver was refined products more than crude: China's suspension of October product exports sent Brent up $4.28 on Thursday, and on Friday European governments agreed to release emergency diesel stocks as part of a G7/IEA plan covering 100 million barrels, which reversed part of the rally.
3. Commercial crude fell 3.2 mb to 424.1 mb, ending two straight builds, which is the bullish reflex, while Cushing rose to about 24.7 mb and refinery utilization edged up to 92.7%, so I still need to split the draw between imports, exports and refinery runs.
4. The Brent-WTI spread stayed wide at $11.14, which suggests the squeeze was in internationally traded barrels and products rather than US inland crude.
5. Gas storage rose 85 Bcf to 3,500 Bcf, about 68 Bcf above the five-year average, with Henry Hub spot at $2.90, and next week I'm watching how quickly the emergency diesel release reaches the physical market and whether China's export suspension holds.
