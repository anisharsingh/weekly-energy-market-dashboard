## What changed and why

1. WTI settled at $103.27 and Brent at $116.72, its highest settlement since April 29; December to December, WTI gained $4.25, or 4.3%, and Brent gained $6.86, or 6.2%, which widened the Brent-WTI spread to $13.45.
2. The table shows a smaller WTI gain of $1.86 because November WTI expired on Oct 20 and the series moved to December, which is why this week carries a roll flag.
3. The rally came as Middle East attacks intensified, including claimed Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites that Saudi authorities had not confirmed, which kept a geopolitical risk premium in prices.
4. EIA's Oct 28 report showed commercial crude down 1.9 mb to 410.6 mb, a fourth straight weekly draw against an expected small build, with gasoline up 3.9 mb to 209.2 mb and distillates down 1.5 mb to 102.8 mb.
5. Gas storage rose 80 Bcf to 3,724 Bcf, 38 Bcf above the five-year average and above the 75 Bcf the market expected, and I'm watching how Brent's December contract behaves as it expires on Oct 30.
