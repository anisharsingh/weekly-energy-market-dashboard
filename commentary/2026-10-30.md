## What changed and why

1. WTI settled at $104.12, up $0.85, or 0.8%, on the week and Brent's expiring December contract settled at $117.64, up $0.92, or 0.8%, so both finished October near six-month highs, with WTI up 15.2% for the month and Brent up 13.6%.
2. Brent's December contract expired on Oct 30 and the January contract settled at $113.43, so the like-for-like reading is December to December, where Brent was up 0.8%.
3. Prices climbed into Friday as supply fears tied to the Strait of Hormuz outweighed diplomatic efforts.
4. EIA's Nov 4 report showed commercial crude down 0.9 mb to 409.8 mb, 4% below the five-year average and a fifth straight weekly draw against an expected build, with refinery utilization up 1.7 points to 94.5% and distillates down 3.6 mb to 99.3 mb, about 13% below the five-year average.
5. Cushing rose 0.3 mb to 27.3 mb and gas storage rose 77 Bcf to 3,801 Bcf, a little below the 81 Bcf the market expected.
