## What changed and why

1. Prices were roughly flat on the week, with WTI at $100.30 and Brent at $103.87, so the story was a high risk premium holding rather than a new directional move.
2. That premium reflects Middle East supply risk: drone attacks on Sept 11 damaged three pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, a route that lets Saudi crude bypass the Strait of Hormuz, and the line only partially restarted around Sept 22.
3. US commercial crude rose 3.0 mb to 426.4 mb, about 2% above the five-year average, which is mildly bearish for WTI, and refinery utilization slipped to about 94% from 96.8%, which fits with less refinery demand for crude.
4. The Brent-WTI spread narrowed by about $1 to $3.57, meaning US crude was keeping pace with seaborne crude for the moment.
5. Gas storage rose 53 Bcf to 3,351 Bcf, and next week I'm watching whether the Saudi pipeline restart and any US-Iran diplomacy take premium out of prices, and which benchmark moves first.
