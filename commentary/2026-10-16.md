## What changed and why

1. WTI jumped $9.56, or 10.4%, to $101.41 and Brent rose $5.14, or 4.9%, to $109.86, its highest settlement since May 19, which narrowed the Brent-WTI spread from $12.87 to $8.45.
2. Reports tied the rally to supply worries around the Persian Gulf and uncertainty over Russian diesel availability, with attacks on Russian oil infrastructure adding to the concern.
3. EIA's inventory report on Oct 21 added support: commercial crude fell 3.1 mb to 412.5 mb, well beyond the draw of roughly 0.8 mb that analysts expected and on top of the previous week's 8.6 mb draw.
4. Gasoline fell 3.0 mb to 205.3 mb while distillates rose 1.7 mb to 104.3 mb, and refinery utilization slipped 0.1 point to 93.4%.
5. Gas storage rose 77 Bcf to 3,644 Bcf, 35 Bcf above the five-year average, so the surplus narrowed from 45 Bcf; November WTI was due to expire on Oct 20, so I compare contracts like-for-like the following week.
